== Ask 1: K-means ==
K-means alternates between two operations: assigning each data point to its nearest centroid and moving each centroid to the mean of its assigned examples [1]. The second operation involves recomputing the centroid by taking the average of all the points assigned to it, and then moving the centroid [2].

Initialization of K-means involves randomly picking $k$ training examples to set as the starting centroids [2]. Repeating initialization is recommended because different initial centroids can result in different local optima [1]. If one initializes K-means with different starting points, it may end at a local minimum [2]. Therefore, repeating the initialization allows for multiple attempts to find the best local optimum [2].

== Ask 2: recommender retrieval and ranking ==
Retrieval and ranking are two steps in a production recommender system [1][3]. Retrieval is the step that gathers a large list of plausible item candidates [2]. This step focuses on coverage, aiming to cover many possible recommendations for the user [2].

The ranking step follows retrieval and fine-tunes the results to select the best items to recommend to the user [2]. While retrieving more items can lead to better performance, it increases computation, so this tradeoff should be tested [1].

In the context of content-based filtering, which involves calculating vectors for users and items to find matches [3], the process is often described as two steps: retrieval and ranking, especially when recommending from a large catalog to scale the system [3]. Offline evaluation can also be used to test the relevance-versus-computation tradeoff in candidate retrieval [4].

== Ask 3: product leadership archetypes ==
The distinction between an Operator and a Craftsperson in product leadership lies in their core strengths and areas of focus [1].

*   **Operator:** This archetype is characterized by strengths in scaling teams, aligning organizations, removing execution barriers, and creating operational clarity [1].
*   **Craftsperson:** This archetype is strong in product definition, customer insight, user experience, strategy, and mentoring product managers [1].

A person combining these archetypes would be skilled at both executing efficiently within an organization (Operator) and ensuring the product itself is well-crafted and user-centric (Craftsperson) [5]. For example, such a leader might streamline team processes while also diving into user research to refine the product’s user experience [5].
