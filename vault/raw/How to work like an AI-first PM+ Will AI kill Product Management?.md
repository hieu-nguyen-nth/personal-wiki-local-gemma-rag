# Will AI kill Product Management?
-  the role will change. But the traits that make a great product manager today will be as important in the future: strong customer empathy, strategic thinking, and leadership skills
- Product Research & Development: from taking to figuring and validating hypothesis, it's not easy to get automated by AI
	- **Product Research** 
		- focuses on gaining new knowledge through investigation and experimentation. It is a loop that starts by talking to customers, reviewing data to confirm a trend, and then running experiments to validate hypotheses. Research should focus on figuring out what to build.
	- **Product Development** 
		- takes the knowledge acquired from research and applies it to create a tangible product, process, or service that translates research findings into a practical application: coordinating the design and development of what a customer wants.
	- Human empathy is important and cant be replace 
	- Humans play a critical role in defining direction and decision–making, and the PM's role as a strategist, visionary, customer researcher, and analyst will not disappear. To do this well, however, requires a level of empathy that most _humans_ struggle with, let alone AI. 
- AI enhances productivity and gets us to the hard part faster -> PM role in customer demand, accretive strategy and coordinate
	- As generative AI improves engineering productivity by 20-50% -> As teams accelerate, the direction-setting that PMs do becomes even more important.
	- It helps us speed through mundane, easy work that previously required a lot of hours of "execution." 
	- But need PMs to push through that hard part — to focus on what customers want, define an accretive strategy, and coordinate AI-amplified people across an organization
	- I don't see the future of product management in the hands of a small number of super ICs. Instead, we'll see product management take on an even more central role — and we'll see demand for what only the most senior and skilled PMs and product leaders can deliver today—deep product sense, rigorous strategic thinking, analytical decision-making, and the ability to build, lead, motivate, and align teams.
