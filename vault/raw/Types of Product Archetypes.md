Shreyas Doshi, a seasoned product management expert, has outlined three distinct archetypes of product leaders: **The Operator**, **The Craftsperson**, and **The Visionary**. These archetypes describe different styles of product leadership, each with unique strengths, weaknesses, and approaches to product management. Below is an explanation of each archetype and how to differentiate them, based on information gathered from web sources, particularly Doshi’s own writings and discussions.

### 1. The Operator

**Explanation**:

- **Core Strength**: Operators excel at scaling teams, ensuring cross-organizational alignment, and unblocking execution. They are adept at managing processes, coordinating with stakeholders, and driving operational efficiency. Their superpower is communication, enabling them to align diverse teams toward common goals
- **Focus**: Operators prioritize organizational structure, success metrics, company goals, and cross-functional support. They thrive in environments where execution and scalability are critical, often in larger or rapidly growing organizations.
- **Characteristics**: They are often promoted early based on their potential to manage complexity and are talent magnets, attracting strong team members due to their ability to create clarity and momentum. They enjoy spending time with peers and company executives, focusing on strategic alignment and operational success.
- **Weaknesses**: Operators may overestimate their product sense or strategic insight and can undervalue the importance of deep product craft or taste. They may struggle with original product innovation or navigating highly ambiguous, creative challenges. (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_as-any-product-scales-the-operator-archetype-activity-7154213681893928960-Tsjb)
- **Example Context**: An Operator might excel at leading a product team at a scaling company like Stripe, ensuring that processes are streamlined and teams are aligned to deliver on a roadmap.

**Differentiation**:

- Operators are process-oriented and focus on “how things get done.” During interviews or discussions, they emphasize questions about team size, org structure, metrics, and cross-functional dynamics.(https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1375491623308550144.html)
- They are less likely to dive deeply into user experience details or long-term visionary ideas, focusing instead on immediate execution and organizational efficiency.

### 2. The Craftsperson

**Explanation**:

- **Core Strength**: Craftspeople are exceptional at defining products and strategies, mentoring other product managers, and delivering high-quality user experiences. Their superpower is product insight, allowing them to deeply understand user needs and craft intuitive solutions.(https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Focus**: They prioritize customers, use cases, product strategy, and user experience (UX) issues. Craftspeople are hands-on, often spending time with their teams and users to refine products and ensure they meet high standards of quality and usability.
- **Characteristics**: Promoted based on talent, Craftspeople make singular product impacts through their attention to detail and ability to mentor others. They thrive in environments where product quality and user satisfaction are paramount, such as early-stage products or companies focused on user-centric design.
- **Weaknesses**: Craftspeople may struggle with navigating large organizational structures or managing the “taxes” of complex organizations, such as politics or bureaucracy. They may also be less comfortable with big-picture, disruptive innovation.(https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Example Context**: A Craftsperson might shine at a company like Airtable, focusing on refining the user experience of a product to make it intuitive and delightful for end users.

**Differentiation**:

- Craftspeople are user- and product-focused, often asking questions about customer pain points, use cases, and UX challenges during discussions. They are less concerned with org structure and more invested in the product’s details and strategy.(https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1375491623308550144.html)
- Unlike Operators, they prioritize depth in product design over scalability, and unlike Visionaries, they focus on immediate user needs rather than long-term disruptive ideas.

### 3. The Visionary

**Explanation**:

- **Core Strength**: Visionaries are exceptional at big-picture thinking and inventing what’s next. Their superpower is seeing possibilities others cannot, enabling them to define bold, innovative product directions (https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Focus**: They concentrate on long-term roadmaps, innovative features, and societal impact. Visionaries are often found in environments where disruption or innovation is critical, such as startups or companies exploring new markets.
- **Characteristics**: Early in their careers, Visionaries can be “hard to manage” due to their unconventional thinking and focus on the future. They often gravitate toward founding startups or leading transformative initiatives within larger organizations. They enjoy spending time with other visionaries or users to explore groundbreaking ideas.
- **Weaknesses**: Visionaries may struggle with people management, scaling teams, or handling operational details without support. Their focus on the future can sometimes lead to overlooking immediate execution needs.(https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Example Context**: A Visionary might lead the development of a revolutionary product like a new AI-driven tool at a startup, setting a bold direction that redefines the market.

**Differentiation**:

- Visionaries stand out by focusing on long-term goals, innovative features, and societal impact during discussions. They ask questions about the company’s vision, future roadmap, and potential for disruption.(https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1375491623308550144.html)
- Unlike Operators, they are less concerned with execution details, and unlike Craftspeople, they prioritize bold ideas over refining existing products.

### How to Differentiate the Archetypes

To distinguish between these archetypes, consider the following approaches, as outlined by Doshi and other sources:

1. **Focus During Discussions** (from):(https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1375491623308550144.html)
    - **Operator**: Asks about org size/structure, success metrics, company goals, and cross-functional support. They are concerned with “how” the organization operates and scales.
    - **Craftsperson**: Focuses on customers, use cases, product strategy, and UX issues. They dive into the “what” and “why” of the product itself.
    - **Visionary**: Emphasizes long-term roadmaps, innovative features, and societal impact. They are driven by the “what’s next” and big-picture possibilities.
2. **Strengths and Weaknesses**:
    - Operators excel in execution and alignment but may lack deep product insight.
    - Craftspeople shine in product definition and user experience but may struggle with large-scale organizational challenges.
    - Visionaries lead with bold ideas but may need support in execution or people management.
3. **Context Fit**:
    - Operators thrive in scaling environments or large organizations with complex coordination needs.
    - Craftspeople are best suited for user-centric or early-stage products where quality and strategy are critical.
    - Visionaries excel in innovative, disruptive settings, such as startups or new market explorations.
4. **Hiring and Self-Assessment** (from,):(https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144) (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_as-any-product-scales-the-operator-archetype-activity-7154213681893928960-Tsjb)
    - When hiring, test Operators for self-awareness, ownership mindset, and ability to retain Craftspeople. Test Craftspeople for their ability to balance strategy and execution. Test Visionaries for their ability to translate bold ideas into actionable plans.
    - For self-assessment, product leaders can reflect on which archetype aligns with their strengths and preferences, recognizing that most embody a primary archetype but may develop secondary traits over time.

### Additional Notes

- **Hybrid Nature**: Doshi notes that most product leaders embody more than one archetype over time but typically retain a primary preference. Think of these as “hats” that leaders wear depending on the context, with one being dominant (https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Hiring Implications**: For founders or CEOs, understanding these archetypes helps in hiring the right product leader for the company’s stage and needs. For PMs, it aids in choosing the right manager or planning their leadership journey.(https://twtr.domain.glass/shreyas/status/1375491623308550144)
- **Complementary Roles**: Organizations often benefit from a mix of these archetypes. For example, Operators can scale what Craftspeople build, while Visionaries set the direction. A strong team balances these strengths, with Operators ensuring execution, Craftspeople ensuring quality, and Visionaries driving innovation.(https://www.linkedin.com/posts/shreyasdoshi_as-any-product-scales-the-operator-archetype-activity-7154213681893928960-Tsjb)

This understanding is crucial for hiring, career planning, and building balanced product teams. Yes, a person can embody both the Operator and Craftsperson archetypes, as outlined by Shreyas Doshi, though they typically have a primary archetype that dominates their approach. According to Doshi’s framework, most product leaders develop traits from multiple archetypes over time, but one tends to be their core strength. Here’s how someone can be both and what it looks like:

### How Someone Can Be Both Operator and Craftsperson

1. **Complementary Strengths**:
    - **Operator Traits**: Focus on scaling teams, aligning cross-functional stakeholders, and driving execution through strong communication and process management.
    - **Craftsperson Traits**: Excel at defining products, mentoring PMs, and delivering high-quality user experiences through deep product insight and attention to user needs.
    - A person combining these archetypes would be skilled at both executing efficiently within an organization (Operator) and ensuring the product itself is well-crafted and user-centric (Craftsperson). For example, they might streamline team processes while also diving into user research to refine the product’s UX.
2. **Contextual Application**:
    - In certain company stages, such as a mid-sized startup scaling rapidly, a product leader might need to wear both hats. They could set up scalable processes (Operator) while also mentoring the team on product strategy and ensuring high-quality outputs (Craftsperson).
    - For instance, at a company like Notion, a product leader might coordinate cross-team efforts to launch new features (Operator) while also obsessing over the user experience to ensure it’s intuitive and polished (Craftsperson).
3. **Evolving Over Time**:
    - Doshi notes that product leaders often grow into secondary archetypes as they gain experience. An Operator might develop Craftsperson skills by working closely with users or mentoring PMs, while a Craftsperson might pick up Operator traits by taking on larger team responsibilities.
    - This hybrid nature is common in senior product leaders who need to balance execution with product quality.

### Differentiating and Identifying the Blend

- **Primary vs. Secondary Archetype**: Someone who is both will likely lean more heavily toward one archetype. For example:
    - A primary Operator with Craftsperson traits might focus on execution but occasionally dive into product details to ensure quality.
    - A primary Craftsperson with Operator traits might prioritize user experience but also excel at aligning teams to deliver on time.
    - You can identify their primary archetype by observing what they naturally gravitate toward in discussions or decisions. For instance, do they spend more time on org structure and metrics (Operator) or user needs and product strategy (Craftsperson)?
- **Questions They Ask**: In meetings or interviews, a hybrid might ask about both org structure and success metrics (Operator) and customer pain points or UX challenges (Craftsperson). For example, they might inquire, “How do we ensure cross-team alignment for this launch, and what user feedback are we incorporating into the design?”
- **Strengths and Weaknesses**: A hybrid might excel at both scaling teams and crafting great products but could still struggle with Visionary tasks, like defining disruptive, long-term strategies. They might also face challenges balancing the two roles if organizational demands pull them too far from product details or vice versa.

### Example Scenario

Imagine a product leader at a company like Figma:

- As an **Operator**, they streamline collaboration between design, engineering, and marketing teams to ensure timely feature releases, setting clear metrics and processes.
- As a **Craftsperson**, they dive into user feedback to refine Figma’s collaborative design tools, ensuring the interface is intuitive and meets user needs.
- This person thrives in a role where they can both manage execution and contribute to product quality, but their primary archetype (e.g., Operator) might show in their preference for process-driven discussions over deep UX debates.

### Conclusion

A person can absolutely be both an Operator and a Craftsperson, blending executional prowess with product insight. The key is identifying their primary archetype through their focus, strengths, and what energizes them most. For career growth or hiring, recognizing this hybrid nature helps place them in roles where they can leverage both skill sets effectively.
